Voting & ballots
Most decisions a Technical Committee (TC) makes are settled by a vote. This page sets out the rules that govern every TC ballot: which majority is the default, who is entitled to vote, how electronic ballots are run, and how a Special Majority Vote is administered. The governing rules live in TC Process §1.11 and the OASIS Defined Terms .
The default vote: Simple Majority Vote
Unless the TC Process specifies otherwise for a particular action, a TC vote passes by Simple Majority Vote . TC Process §1.11
A Simple Majority Vote is a vote in which the number of "yes" votes cast is greater than the number of "no" votes cast. Abstentions are not counted. OASIS Defined Terms
Higher thresholds apply where the TC Process names them. The two that recur in the work-product lifecycle are the Full Majority Vote (more than 50% of all eligible voters vote "yes") and the Special Majority Vote (at least 2/3 vote "yes" and no more than 1/4 vote "no"). OASIS Defined Terms The table below compares all three.
Vote types at a glance
Comparison of the three TC vote types and their passing thresholds
Vote type Passes when Where defined
Simple Majority Vote
More "yes" votes than "no" votes. Abstentions are not counted. This is the default for any action the TC Process does not assign a higher threshold.
OASIS Defined Terms
Full Majority Vote
More than 50% (more than half) of the eligible voters vote "yes", whether present or not. Abstentions are not counted.
OASIS Defined Terms
Special Majority Vote
At least 2/3 (two thirds) of the eligible voters vote "yes" and no more than 1/4 (one fourth) vote "no". These numbers are based on the total number of eligible voters. Abstentions are not counted.
OASIS Defined Terms
Thresholds are counted against eligible voters , not against the number present. For the Full Majority Vote , a Voting Member who does not vote has the same effect as a "no", because the threshold (more than half of all eligible voters) is counted against the full roster. The Special Majority Vote works differently: it has two prongs (at least 2/3 of eligible voters must vote Yes and no more than 1/4 may vote No), so a non-vote is not equivalent to a No on the second prong, where only an actual No vote counts toward the 1/4 limit.
Who may vote
Policy requirement
Every Voting Member of a TC has a single vote . Organizations do not vote in TCs : voting is by the individual Voting Member, not by their company. TC Process §1.11
Eligibility is fixed when the ballot opens. A Member of a TC must have voting rights at the time a ballot is opened in order to vote on that ballot. Gaining voting rights after the ballot opens does not let a Member vote on it. TC Process §1.11
No proxies. Proxies shall not be allowed in TC voting. A Voting Member cannot delegate or transfer their vote to anyone else. TC Process §1.11
For how voting rights are gained, lost, and regained, see Gain voting rights and Lose / regain voting rights .
Electronic ballots
TCs may conduct electronic ballots. The rules differ depending on whether the ballot is held during a meeting or outside of one. TC Process §1.11

Policy requirement
Any Work Product Ballot conducted as an electronic ballot must permit each voter to choose "yes", "no", or "abstain" . TC Process §1.11
Changing a vote
While a ballot is open, eligible voters may change their vote up until the end of the voting period . Once the ballot closes, votes are final. TC Process §1.11
Special Majority Vote administration
Some actions require a Special Majority Vote: approving a Committee Specification, submitting a Candidate OASIS Standard, and clarifying or rechartering a TC. Those ballots are not run by the TC itself.
Policy requirement
All TC ballots requiring a Special Majority Vote must be conducted by the TC Administrator . The TC Chair shall request that the TC Administrator hold the Special Majority Vote, and the TC Administrator shall set up and conduct the ballot . TC Process §1.11 OASIS Defined Terms
A Special Majority Vote is therefore conducted by the OASIS TC Administrator, with the thresholds counted against the total number of eligible voters as shown in the comparison table above. OASIS Defined Terms
