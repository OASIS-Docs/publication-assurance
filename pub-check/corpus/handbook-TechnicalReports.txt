Technical Reports
A Technical Report (TR) is a Non-Standards Track work product produced by a Technical Committee under one or more named Authors. It is approved by Full Majority Vote . A Technical Report Draft (TRD) is the in-progress form of a Technical Report before TC approval. Unlike Standards Track work products, Technical Reports are not eligible to become an OASIS Standard.
What changed since the 2010 handbook
Technical Reports and Technical Report Drafts are a new work product type with no equivalent in the 2010 handbook. They were introduced as defined terms in the OASIS Defined Terms effective 1 December 2020 . The 2010 handbook covered only Committee Notes on the Non-Standards Track. If your reference material makes no mention of Technical Reports, it predates this type.
Where this sits in the lifecycle
Working Draft
Editors develop the work product incrementally as a Working Draft before it is designated a Technical Report Draft. Working Drafts are editor-controlled and do not require a TC ballot. A TC may also approve a work product directly as a Technical Report from Working Draft without first passing a TRD designation ballot, where no public review is needed. Document Life Cycle Best Practices
Technical Report Draft Full Majority Vote
The TC designates work product material as a Technical Report Draft by Full Majority Vote , meaning more than 50% of all eligible voters vote Yes, whether present or not. OASIS Defined Terms, Technical Report Draft (Def. 63)
Public review (optional)
Public review is optional for Technical Reports: "a public review is not required for a Technical Report." If the TC chooses to hold one, the review must be opened by the TC and comments addressed before the final approval ballot. OASIS Defined Terms, Technical Report (Def. 62)
Technical Report Full Majority Vote
The TC approves the work product as a Technical Report by Full Majority Vote . OASIS Defined Terms, Technical Report (Def. 62)
Key characteristics
Authors, not just editors
A Technical Report has one or more named Authors . This distinguishes it from Committee Notes, which list editors. Authorship is recorded on the cover page and is part of the definition of the work product type. OASIS Defined Terms, Technical Report (Def. 62)
Full Majority Vote threshold
Policy requirement
Both the TRD designation and the final TR approval require a Full Majority Vote : more than 50% of all eligible Voting Members must vote Yes, counting all eligible voters whether or not they are present at the meeting. OASIS Defined Terms, Full Majority Vote
Public review is optional
There is no requirement to hold a public review before approving a Technical Report: the Defined Terms state that "a public review is not required for a Technical Report." OASIS Defined Terms, Technical Report (Def. 62) Conducting a public review is at the TC's discretion and is an intrinsic feature of the work product type, not a standing rule. If the TC chooses to hold a public review, it must follow the same process as defined for a CSD, including use of the TC's public comment facility. Document Life Cycle Best Practices, Public review process
Non-Standards Track only
Technical Reports are Non-Standards Track work products. They are informational in nature and cannot be submitted as a candidate for an OASIS Standard. The current Non-Standards Track work product types are: Committee Note Draft (CND), Committee Note (CN), Technical Report Draft (TRD), and Technical Report (TR). OASIS Defined Terms, OASIS Non-Standards Draft Deliverable; OASIS Non-Standards Final Deliverable

Historical note
The definitive authority for Technical Reports is the OASIS Defined Terms (effective 1 December 2020) and the Document Life Cycle Best Practices (2024.08.22). These documents define the TRD and TR types, their approval votes, and their lifecycle.
The TC Process §2.4.2 , which enumerates Non-Standards Track work product types, currently lists only Committee Notes in that section. The text of TC Process §2.4.2 has not yet been fully harmonized to incorporate TRD and TR explicitly by name. Until harmonization is complete, practitioners should rely on the Defined Terms and lifecycle best practices as the primary authority for Technical Reports, and treat TC Process §2.4.2 as providing only partial coverage of the Non-Standards Track.
