Committee Specifications
A Committee Specification (CS) is the highest level of approval a Technical Committee can grant to its own work product. It requires a Special Majority Vote of the TC and may only be held after a successful public review in which all comments have been resolved .
Common misconception
After a TC approves CS01 (its first Committee Specification), the work product does not return to the Working Draft stage. To make substantive changes, the TC revises and re-approves at the CSD stage and runs a new public review; it does not drop back to a Working Draft.
The Standards Track is forward-only and no step may be skipped TC Process §2.4.1 ; revision is done by re-approving a CSD any number of times TC Process §2.5 and re-running public review on that CSD TC Process §2.6 . The TC Process defines no path that returns an approved CS to Working Draft. TC Process §2.7
What changed since the 2010 handbook
Two important corrections from the old handbook:
No CSPRD stage. The old handbook described a distinct "Committee Specification Public Review Draft (CSPRD)" stage and file-naming designation. That stage abbreviation was removed in Naming Directives v1.7 (effective 2 January 2024). A document sent to public review is still a Committee Specification Draft (CSD); no separate stage name or abbreviation is applied to the public-review copy.
Statements of Use are NOT required here. The old handbook placed the three-Statements-of-Use requirement at the Committee Specification stage. Under the current TC Process, Statements of Use are required only when the TC submits a CS as a Candidate OASIS Standard ( TC Process §2.8.1 ). A CS may be approved by the TC without any Statements of Use.
Where this sits in the lifecycle
Committee Specification Draft csd Full Majority Vote
The TC approves a Working Draft as a Committee Specification Draft by Full Majority Vote. TC Process §2.5
Public Review of the CSD Full Majority Vote to open
The TC opens a public review by Full Majority Vote. The first public review must run for at least 30 days ; any subsequent public review of the same work product must run for at least 15 days . All comments received are formally tracked and must be resolved. TC Process §2.6
Committee Specification cs Special Majority Vote
The TC approves the CS by Special Majority Vote, only after a successful public review and resolution of all comments. TC Process §2.7
Candidate OASIS Standard (optional next step) Special Majority Vote
If the TC later seeks to elevate the CS to an OASIS Standard, it submits the CS as a Candidate by Special Majority Vote; at that point three Statements of Use are required. TC Process §2.8.1
The approval vote
Policy requirement
A Committee Specification may be approved only after all of the following preconditions are satisfied:
A public review has been conducted and has closed successfully.
All comments received during the public review have been resolved to the TC's satisfaction.
The TC holds a Special Majority Vote conducted by electronic ballot by the TC Administrator. TC Process §2.7
Special Majority Vote: what the threshold means
A Special Majority Vote passes only when at least 2/3 of eligible Voting Members vote Yes AND no more than 1/4 vote No . Abstentions and non-votes are not counted as Yes or No but they are included in the fixed denominator: the "eligible voters" figure is the total roster of Voting Members, whether they participate or not. The vote is always conducted by electronic ballot administered by the TC Administrator; it cannot be taken in a meeting by a show of hands. Defined Terms: Special Majority Vote
Resolving all comments before the ballot
The TC Process requires that all comments from the public review be resolved before the CS approval ballot opens. TC Process §2.7 In practice, the TC Chair and editor maintain a Comment Resolution Log documenting the disposition of every comment (accepted, rejected with rationale, or incorporated). The ballot announcement should reference this log.
Statements of Use: do not confuse the stages
Policy requirement
Three Statements of Use (at least one from an OASIS Organizational Member) are required when the TC submits a Committee Specification as a Candidate OASIS Standard . TC Process §2.8.1 They are not a precondition for approving a Committee Specification itself.
A TC may approve a Committee Specification without any Statements of Use. Gathering Statements of Use is a separate activity that the TC pursues if it intends to advance the CS toward an OASIS Standard; the requirement applies at the COS submission ballot, not at the CS approval ballot.
Naming and URIs after approval
Once approved, the work product carries the stage abbreviation cs followed by a revision number: for example, myspec-v1.0-cs01.html . Naming Directives v1.7
The abbreviation csprd is obsolete and must not be used . It was removed in Naming Directives v1.7 (effective 2 January 2024). A document undergoing public review is still named as a CSD (e.g., csd02 ).
A CS may be approved at revision 01 (first CS) or higher if the TC voted to re-approve after substantive changes. Each re-approval requires a new Special Majority Vote ballot.
Published resources in the OASIS Library must not be deleted or altered after publication; only "latest stage" aliases may be updated. Naming Directives v1.7
What comes next
Once a CS is approved, the TC has three options:
Stop here. Many specifications are published as Committee Specifications and remain at that level permanently. A CS is a complete, published, citable OASIS work product.
Advance to OASIS Standard. The TC may vote (by Special Majority Vote) to submit the CS as a Candidate OASIS Standard, triggering the 60-day public review and Call for Consent process. See Approving an OASIS Standard .
Revise and re-approve. If substantive changes are required, the TC re-approves the revision at the CSD stage (not by reverting to a Working Draft), conducts a new (or additional) public review, and holds a new Special Majority Vote. TC Process §2.5
